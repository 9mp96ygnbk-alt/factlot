## What the statute says

A.R.S. § 39-121.03, https://www.azleg.gov/ars/39/00121-03.htm (read 2026-11-03). It is not silent on commercial use. It requires a statement, allows a fee that can include commercial-market value, allows the governor to prohibit a purpose the custodian calls misuse, and sets treble damages for a mismatch. It does not itself ban redistribution once the purpose is stated and any fee is paid.

> When a person requests copies, printouts or photographs of public records for a commercial purpose, the person shall provide a statement setting forth the commercial purpose for which the copies, printouts or photographs will be used.

> shall in addition to other penalties be liable to the state or the political subdivision from which the public record was obtained for damages in the amount of three times the amount which would have been charged

> or for any purpose in which the purchaser can reasonably anticipate the receipt of monetary gain from the direct or indirect use of the public record.

> Commercial purpose does not mean the use of a public record as evidence or as research for evidence in an action in any judicial or quasi-judicial body.

Publishing cited facts, and charging for hosted access to them, is a reasonably anticipated monetary gain. It is not the evidence exception.

## Options

1. Use the records after the founder signs FOR-025 and the office has the statement. The draft PDF is that statement.
2. Send the short cover in `az-maricopa-assessor-letter.md` with the form. The form, not the letter, is the statutory statement.
3. Do not load.

## Recommendation

Option 1. Replace the address, email, and phone. Sign and date the certification and the pricing worksheet. Leave the receipt signature blank until delivery. Tick the not-to-exceed box only if a cap of 0 is acceptable. Leave the deposit box blank unless the office quotes a fee. Do not load the three zip files until the office has the signed statement. Do not resell the files. Do not store natural-person names.

## Risk if wrong

If the true purpose is left off the statement, subsection (C) allows damages of three times the charge that would have applied, plus costs and attorney fees, or three times actual damages if the office would have refused. An unstated hosted fee is that kind of gap. If the office sets a commercial-market price, paying nothing is not compliance. The governor may also bar a purpose the custodian calls misuse.
