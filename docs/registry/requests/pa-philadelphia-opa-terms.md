# Philadelphia Office of Property Assessment (`pa-philadelphia-opa`): terms decision memo

Date read: 2026-11-03 (earlier captures 2026-11-02 as marked). Prepared for: the founder.
This memo is research, not legal advice; the founder holds no attorney or real estate license.

## What the publisher's terms say

The dataset page https://opendataphilly.org/datasets/philadelphia-properties-and-assessment-history/ (read 2026-11-03, HTTP 200) is titled "Philadelphia Properties and Assessment History". The saved HTML links the bulk file at `https://opendata-downloads.s3.amazonaws.com/opa_properties_public.csv`.

The City's terms, https://metadata.phila.gov/#help/help-faqs/what-are-the-terms-of-use/ (read 2026-11-02), include "Browsing City data on this site, on opendataphilly.org, or on any other repository that" and "City's terms of use and your agreement to be bound by them." They also include "The City of Philadelphia reserves all rights in the database and any data contained therein," and "receive, any title or interest in the database or any other City data." The same capture has an as-is disclaimer and a hold-harmless clause. Those sentences grant no redistribution right. AI use is not addressed.

OpenDataPhilly, https://opendataphilly.org/about/ (read 2026-11-03), says "The creator of the data sets, applications, and APIs retain any copyright or intellectual property restrictions they have placed on the data sets, applications, or APIs." It also says "By using data linked to on OpenDataPhilly, the user also agrees to any additional terms of use or conditions defined by the organization or individual who created the data set, application, or API." It does not add a non-commercial limit.

The department address in the working notes (read 2026-11-02), https://www.phila.gov/departments/office-of-property-assessment/, includes "601 Walnut St., Suite 300 W., Philadelphia, PA 19106" and `opadata@phila.gov`. The City Open Records Policy, https://www.phila.gov/open-records-policy/ (read 2026-11-03), lists "601 Walnut St, Ste 300 West, Philadelphia, PA 19106" and "RighttoKnowOPA@phila.gov" for the Office of Property Assessment. The same page lists "1515 Arch St., 15th Fl, Philadelphia, PA 19102" and "RightToKnowLaw@phila.gov" for the Law Department Open Records Officer.

## What the statute says

Pennsylvania Right-to-Know Law, Act 3 of 2008. Source URL https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/US/PDF/2008/0/0003..PDF (fetched 2026-11-02). This lane read the text already saved with the repository and did not re-fetch the PDF. Section 302(b) is wrapped in that text as "A local agency may not deny a requester", "access to a public record due to the intended use of the public", and "record by the requester unless otherwise provided by law."

The working notes (read 2026-11-02) quote section 701(a) as "record shall be accessible for inspection and duplication in accordance with this act. A record" and "being provided to a requester shall be provided in the medium requested if it exists in that". Section 703: "A written request need not include any explanation of the requester's reason for" and "requesting or intended use of the records unless otherwise required by law." Section 1308(2) in the saved text: "A requirement to disclose the purpose or motive in" and "requesting access to records."

The notes state: "Nothing in the act restricts commercial use or redistribution of records obtained under it." The sections read here are silent on redistribution after the record is obtained. They are not silent on intended use at the point of access: section 302(b) says a local agency may not deny access because of that use, unless another law says so.

## Options

1. Use as a public record.
2. Ask the publisher for a written statement (letter: `pa-philadelphia-opa-letter.md`).
3. Do not load.

## Recommendation

Send the letter to the Office of Property Assessment and leave `verified` false until the City answers or you decide that the Right-to-Know Law controls. The download itself is open. The City's terms reserve all rights in the database and state no redistribution right. The statute, in the sections read, refuses to let intended use be a reason to withhold the record, and it does not grant a license to republish the database.

## Risk if wrong

If the rights reservation controls, publishing derived facts could be treated as a use the City did not transfer. If the statute controls and the registry waits, Philadelphia commercial-parcel facts stay unpublished even though the file is already on an open-data site. The terms are a browse-wrap notice: the page says browsing the data constitutes acceptance.
