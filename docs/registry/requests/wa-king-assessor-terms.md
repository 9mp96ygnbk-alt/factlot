That sentence does not authorize publication. The Restrictions sentence names "data" and forbids publishing, displaying, distributing, or commercially exploiting it without prior written permission. On its face, that clause is not compatible with an open registry.

## What the statute says

RCW 42.56.070, https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=42.56.070 (read 2026-11-03). The acknowledgement cites subsection (9). The current text numbers the commercial-list rule as (8). NOTES.md already noted that (read 2026-11-02).

> Each agency, in accordance with published rules, shall make available for public inspection and copying all public records, unless the record falls within the specific exemptions of subsection (8) of this section, this chapter, or other statute which exempts or prohibits disclosure of specific information or records.

> This chapter shall not be construed as giving authority to any agency, the office of the secretary of the senate, or the office of the chief clerk of the house of representatives to give, sell or provide access to lists of individuals requested for commercial purposes, and agencies, the office of the secretary of the senate, and the office of the chief clerk of the house of representatives shall not do so unless specifically authorized or directed by law:

> PROVIDED FURTHER, That such recognition may be refused only for a good cause pursuant to a hearing under the provisions of chapter 34.05 RCW, the administrative procedure act.

The history line says "Formerly RCW 42.17.260."

Subsection (8) restricts lists of individuals requested for commercial purposes. It is silent on commercial use or redistribution of records that are not such lists. It does not grant permission to republish website data. The registry stores no natural-person names, which matches the subject of (8), and does not answer the Restrictions clause.

## Options

1. Use as a public record, tick the box, and publish. That treats the Restrictions clause as if it did not apply. The clause does not say that.
2. Ask for written permission. The letter is `wa-king-assessor-letter.md`. Do not tick the box.
3. Do not load.

## Recommendation

Option 2. The Restrictions clause is not compatible, on its face, with publishing or distributing website content that includes data, without prior written permission. The acknowledgement cites subsection (9), speaks only to lists of individuals, and was not accepted. Do not download until the County answers the letter. If it refuses, use option 3.

## Risk if wrong

Publishing without written permission gives the County a basis to claim a breach of terms that call the content, including data, County property and that require permission to publish, display, distribute, or commercially exploit it. If a list of individuals were used commercially, the acknowledgement says that use may bring liability, and subsection (8) tells agencies not to release such lists. A hosted service that charges for derived facts is a commercial setting even if the zip file is not resold.
