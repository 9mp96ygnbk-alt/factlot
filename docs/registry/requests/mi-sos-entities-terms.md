## What the statute says

MCL 15.233, https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-15-233 , as quoted in NOTES.md (read 2026-11-02). It was not re-fetched. A person has a right to inspect or receive copies of an existing public record described well enough to find. A requester that is not an individual must name an individual agent:

> the person's agent who is an individual.

> inspect, copy, or receive copies of the requested public record of the public body.

> A subscription is valid for up to 6 months, at the request of the subscriber, and is renewable.

NOTES.md says subsections (4) and (5) do not require a compilation or a new record, and that no section read addresses commercial use or redistribution. On commercial use and redistribution, the statute as captured is silent. MCL 15.231(2), quoted in the same notes, states the public-policy entitlement to information about government affairs. The request asks only for an existing extract of entity identifier, legal name, status, type, formation date, and organisation address. It does not ask for officer or member names. The founder signs as FactLot's individual agent and must supply a postal address and a phone or email before sending.

## Options

1. Use a published bulk file. None was identified on the pages read.
2. Send the FOIA draft and wait for a cost estimate. Do not start deposit-level processing until the founder accepts the estimate in writing.
3. Do not load.

## Recommendation

Option 2. Fill the address, phone, and email, sign, and email LARAFOIAInfo@michigan.gov or mail the FOIA Coordinator at the Ottawa Building box above. Do not use the portal. If the Department says no extract exists, stop. Do not ask it to build one.

## Risk if wrong

A request that asks for a new compilation can be denied, and a cost estimate over fifty dollars requires a deposit before processing. Paying that deposit, or letting the clock run, is a founder decision this draft does not make. Using the portal would create an account, which this lane must not do. Publishing natural-person officer names would break the registry rule even if a file contained them. The statute, as read, does not bless or forbid commercial redistribution, so a silent statute is not a licence to ignore a later written condition the Department might attach to a copy.
