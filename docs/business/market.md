## 1. Who could buy a CRE fact API

FactLot sells facts about commercial property (parcels, owners, entities, lease terms, rules)
to software, including AI agents, that does commercial real estate work. The buyer is an
organisation that runs such software, either one it builds (a lender's underwriting agent, a
brokerage's research tool) or one it buys from a vendor who embeds FactLot. The counts below
are organisations, not people, because pricing is per account and per call, never per seat.

### 1.2 TAM, SAM, SOM (bottom-up)

**Method.** Count the organisations in each segment, put each into the FactLot plan its size
fits (Pro under 20 employees, Team 20–99, Enterprise 100+ or institutional), multiply by that
plan's annual price from the [financial model](model.md), and sum. This gives the revenue if
every organisation bought. Then apply two filters for the serviceable market, and take the
model's own 36-month revenue as the obtainable share.

**Plan prices used (assumptions, from the model):** Pro $79 per month average ($948 a year),
which is the $49 base plus typical registry overage; Team $850 per month average ($10,200 a
year), the $599 base plus overage; Enterprise $48,000 a year.

**TAM, every counted organisation at the plan its size fits.** Brokerage is counted by NAR's
commercial-primary members (34,329) for the Pro tier instead of all 531210 firms, because the
Census count is mostly residential; its 20+ and 100+ size classes are kept because large
brokerages all have commercial arms. Lenders are counted once each: banks with CRE loans (about
4,150), credit unions with commercial loans (1,778), life insurers (711), and the MBA-ranked
originators (129); the 536 concentration banks are a subset of the 4,150 and are placed in the
Enterprise tier. Overlaps between segments (a REIT that is also a lessor firm) are not removed,
so the total is an upper bound.

**SAM, the part FactLot can serve in the next three years.** Two filters, both **assumptions**:

- **Geography, 30%.** FactLot's registry covers the five largest Texas counties, New York City,
  Los Angeles, San Francisco, Boston, Philadelphia, Denver, Hennepin, Mecklenburg, and the
  District of Columbia (708,538 commercial parcels; see the
  [source catalog](../registry/sources/README.md)). No public figure gives the share of US
  commercial property or of CRE firms in those metros; 30% is a placeholder for "most of the
  largest dozen metros" and must be replaced with a count from the registry itself once parcel
  counts are compared with a national parcel file (ReportAll counts 161.7 million parcels of all
  kinds across 3,230 counties, read 2026-11-03, [link](https://reportallusa.com/about-our-data)).
- **Agent adoption, 25% of Team and Enterprise organisations and 10% of Pro organisations run or
  buy an AI agent that needs cited facts within 36 months.** No cited figure supports a specific
  share; this is the single largest unknown and the first thing the 54 interviews should measure.

**SOM, what the model says FactLot reaches by month 36:** $0.85M ARR in the base case (2.3% of
SAM), $0.16M in the low case, $4.1M in the high case (11% of SAM). See [model.md](model.md).

**What would change these numbers most.** (1) A real geography share from the registry's own
parcel counts against a national file. (2) A measured adoption rate from interviews. (3) Whether
PropTech vendors embed FactLot and pay per call on behalf of their customers, which would move
thousands of Pro-tier organisations into one Team or Enterprise contract each; the model treats
vendors as ordinary Team accounts. (4) Owner-name gaps in the loaded sources (Los Angeles, San
Francisco) that cut the Enterprise value of the registry in those metros until they are closed.

**What this says about FactLot's position.**

- **Nobody publishes a per-record citation to the source document and page.** Regrid comes
  closest with field-level lineage and a county URL where available; Green Street returns report
  links. FactLot's fact model, where every fact carries a snapshot document ID, record locator,
  method, and rights, is not matched by any vendor above.
- **AI-use terms are tightening, not loosening.** Regrid, LightBox, Altus, Green Street, and
  CoStar (per the filing) all restrict AI training or AI access in writing. This is the 35% of
  simulated personas who said licensing blocks them; it needs confirming with real buyers, but
  the vendors' own terms are evidence that the problem exists.
- **MCP servers are now table stakes for data vendors.** ATTOM (Jan 2026), Regrid (Aug 2026),
  and Green Street (Aug 2026) all ship one. FactLot's distribution advantage is not "has an MCP
  server"; it is that the calculators, IDs, and schema are free and open, and that the registry
  is the only one whose facts are citable and whose rights are machine-readable.
- **The parcel-data market has a price floor.** Regrid's last published nationwide bulk price
  was $50,000 (Standard) and ReportAll sells counties at $250. FactLot's public-record registry
  is not a parcel-geometry product and should not price against them; it should price against
  identifier and lookup services (Placekey, Google Geocoding, Melissa, Smarty), which is where
  per-call prices are public (section 3).

**Directory and platform counts** (why now, cited): the official MCP registry held about 30,375
unique servers in a full crawl on 2026-09-10 ([dev.to](https://dev.to/amareswer/the-mcp-registry-by-the-numbers-38nc),
read 2026-11-03), up from under 10,000 in May 2026; Glama lists 114,874 servers
([glama.ai](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers), read 2026-11-03); GitHub shows 43,100 public
repositories tagged `mcp-server` ([topic page](https://github.com/topics/mcp-server), read

**Per-seat pricing is excluded** on purpose. Of the simulated personas (not evidence), 42%
preferred per-call pricing and per-seat pricing was a frequent dealbreaker; more importantly,
revenue should rise with agent calls, not headcount, because that is where the cost and the
value are.

**Prices that need validating first:** the $0.005 registry call (is a cited owner-of-record fact
worth a geocode, or ten?), the $48,000 Enterprise floor (H3 in the
[customer discovery plan](../customer-discovery.md) sets the kill signal at no buyer at $2,000 a
month), and whether PropTech vendors want a reseller rate.
