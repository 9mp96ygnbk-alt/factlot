The open fact layer for commercial real estate AI agents

AI agents are fluent about commercial real estate, and often wrong about the facts.

FactLot gives them cited facts and deterministic math they can't get wrong: 22 calculators with audit trails, canonical IDs, and a public-record registry where every fact points at the document it came from.

Three layers

Tools

22 deterministic calculators: rent schedules, net effective rent, proposal comparison, percentage rent, CAM reconciliation, debt sizing, ASC 842 remeasurement, prepayment and defeasance, WALT, NPV, IRR, lease vs. own, renew vs. relocate, hotel metrics, property DCF pro forma, direct cap, loan amortization, LP/GP equity waterfalls, 1031 exchange checks, multifamily rent rolls, capital expenditure recovery, and building-wide CAM. Every result carries steps (the audit trail), warnings, and a versioned formula ID such as calc.net_effective_rent@1.

Stable FactLot IDs for parcels, legal entities, buildings (from the street address), spaces, and documents, computable offline; leases get IDs minted by a registry. A fact schema in which every claim carries its provenance (document, page, clause, method) and its AI-use rights.

Rules and document verification

Rules packs turn jurisdiction law into code. Each pack cites its statute, carries a version, and stays draft until a maintainer has checked it against the official text; the pack records who checked it and when. One pack is verified today: the Texas property-tax protest deadline (checked 2026-11-02).

Document verification checks what your agent reads from a lease. Your agent extracts each term with its verbatim quote and page; FactLot confirms the quote is on that page and states the value ("$1,250,000.00", "June 30, 2031", "five percent (5%)") and returns a cited fact, or the reason it could not. It also builds a current-state abstract with amendment precedence, compares an estoppel or rent roll to the lease, and checks a single claim. No model runs on our side, and the checks are textual: they do not find terms your agent missed. Live on the hosted API; nothing is stored. It also spreads a T-12 onto a published chart of accounts with every line cited, checks a proposed use against a center's exclusives, and packages verified facts so a counterparty can check them, signed with your own key.

How it works

An agent calls a tool instead of doing the math in context, then reports the formula and points to the steps. The inputs and values below are the project's shared test vector for net effective rent (spec/vectors/calc/net_effective_rent.json), which both the TypeScript and Python SDKs must reproduce to the last digit. The steps array is elided.

Open source, the Supabase way

The code is free and self-hostable, which matters when leases are confidential. The hosted services that cost real money to run are what we charge for.

  • Schema and SDKs: @factlot/schema and the client SDKs. MIT, free.
  • Engine: calculators, IDs, the MCP server, the remote connector, and the ingest and registry code. Apache-2.0, free, self-hostable.
  • FactLot Cloud: the hosted registry, ingest, and rules packs, with SLAs and support. Usage-based with a free tier, no per-seat pricing. Not yet public.

Business model · Roadmap

Principles

  • Public records only, obtained ethically. Official bulk downloads, open-data portals, public APIs within their terms, or a records request. No circumventing access controls, no scraping where terms forbid it, no data whose licence forbids redistribution or AI use.
  • No natural-person names. The registry keeps commercial parcels and the organisations that own them. A person's name is never stored or indexed, and withheld owner records are never recovered or inferred.
  • Everything cited. Uncited facts are rejected: extracted, public-record and inferred facts must name a source, and computed facts their formula. Each fact names its method, its source document and record, and the rights that travel with it.
  • Deterministic. The TypeScript and Python SDKs produce the same result, checked by shared test vectors and a differential test that fails on any difference, down to the last digit.
