What it computes

Weighted average lease term (WALT) is the average time left on a group of leases, weighted by each lease's size or income. It measures rollover exposure for a building or portfolio and is used in valuation, credit underwriting and asset management reporting. The calculator returns WALT in years from an as_of date, weighted by leased area or by annual rent. It also returns the total weight and the number of leases already expired.

Method and conventions

For each lease, remaining term in years is max(0, days from as_of to expiration_date ÷ 365.25). The days are whole calendar days, the actual count between the two dates. WALT is Σ(weight × years remaining) ÷ Σ weight, reported to four decimals. The weight is area_sf when weight_by is area (the default) and annual_rent when it is rent. Every lease needs the field that matches the chosen weighting.

A lease that expires on or before as_of counts as zero years remaining but keeps its weight, so it lowers WALT. It is counted in expired_count, and the result carries a warning. To leave an expired lease out of the average, omit it from leases.

Practitioners differ in several ways:

  • Weighting. Area weighting treats every square foot equally. Rent weighting gives more influence to leases that pay more, so the two can differ a lot in a building with mixed rents. FactLot computes one at a time and the choice is made with weight_by.
  • Expiry or break date. Some reports measure to the first tenant termination option instead of contractual expiration; this is sometimes called the weighted average lease term to break. FactLot measures to the date given in expiration_date, so a break-date figure uses break dates as that input.
  • Year length. Remaining terms can be counted in months ÷ 12, days ÷ 365, or days ÷ 365.25. FactLot uses actual days ÷ 365.25.
  • Expired and vacant space. Some methods drop expired leases or include vacant space at zero. FactLot keeps expired leases at zero years with their weight. Vacant space enters only if it is listed as a lease.
  • Which rent. Rent weighting can use passing rent, contractual rent over the remaining term, or gross rent with recoveries. FactLot uses whatever annual_rent is supplied for each lease.

Inputs that matter

  • as_of: the date remaining term is measured from, YYYY-MM-DD.
  • weight_by: area (default) or rent.
  • leases: the leases in the building or portfolio, up to 10,000.
  • leases[].expiration_date: the date each lease ends, or the break date for a to-break figure.
  • leases[].area_sf: leased square feet; required for every lease when weighting by area.
  • leases[].annual_rent: current annual rent in dollars; required for every lease when weighting by rent.
  • leases[].id: an optional identifier to keep the result tied to a rent roll.

Limits

  • It does not read options, so renewals, early terminations and month-to-month holdovers are reflected only through the dates supplied.
  • A lease that starts after as_of is measured from as_of. The time before it commences is counted as remaining term.
  • Rent is not escalated or projected. Each lease's weight is the single figure given.
  • Weights must be positive. A lease in a free-rent period cannot be weighted at zero rent.
  • It returns one WALT for one date. It does not produce an expiration schedule by year or a WALT history over time.
