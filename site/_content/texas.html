FactLot's hosted registry holds commercial parcels from six Texas counties, with cited roll values (one roll year per county today), organisation owners only, and the Texas Comptroller entities that own a hosted parcel. Alongside it are tools for the protest-deadline rule and the unequal-appraisal arithmetic.

FactLot is software. It is not a property tax consultant under Texas Occupations Code chapter 1152, does not prepare, file or advise on protests, and does not represent owners before an appraisal review board. If you provide property tax services to others for compensation, your own registration obligations apply.

Each of the six counties holds one roll year. Every fact cites the roll it was read from. Tarrant County is not served by the hosted registry while the publisher's terms for AI use are unclear. registry_coverage lists exactly what is hosted, and the coverage map shows every county.

Tools

  • registry_get: a parcel, legal entity or building by FactLot ID, with its status, current cited facts and ownership links.
  • registry_tx_equity_candidates (and its batch form): candidate comparables for a subject parcel under a stated default rule, returned in the input shape of the calculator below for a person to review and edit.
  • calc_tx_unequal_appraisal: the arithmetic of Tax Code §41.43(b)(3) and §42.26(a)(3) over comparables the person chose, with every step shown.
  • rules_tx_property_tax_protest_deadline: the protest deadline computed from the statute text, with citations. See the Texas property tax protest deadline page.
  • report_create kinds tx_protest_deadline (a calendar or PDF of the deadline) and tx_unequal_appraisal (an evidence packet as a PDF, or the Texas workbook, an XLSX with live Excel formulas beside FactLot's numbers), built by code with no model.

Registry lookups and reports are metered by plan; see pricing.

What this is and is not

These outputs are not an appraisal or an opinion of value (Occupations Code §1103.003(1)), not legal or tax advice, and not a substitute for the advice of an attorney. They do not say whether to protest, what value to put forward, or how a hearing may go. FactLot does not file protests, sign agent designations under §1.111, or contact appraisal districts. Filing is done through the appraisal district's own process, by the owner or by a property tax consultant registered with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, or an attorney. See the calculators.
