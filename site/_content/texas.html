For property tax consultants, tax attorneys, CPAs and in-house tax staff. The tools are software: you choose the comparables and make the argument.

What you get

  • Roll values by year. Market value, and appraised value where the district's file gives it, for each roll year FactLot holds, each naming the district file and record it came from. {{ROLL_SENTENCE}} The current roll year is free; every earlier year needs a Pro or Team key.
  • Candidate comparables under a stated rule. The subject's neighbourhood code first where the roll gives one, then its ZIP code, then the rest of the county only if fewer than 10 are found, within the same state property category and bands of improvement area and year built. The rule is printed beside the list. The candidates are for you to accept, remove or replace.
  • The arithmetic, step by step. The calculator works over the comparables you chose, with your adjustments and their reasons, and returns every step.
  • A workbook and a packet. The Texas workbook (XLSX) and a PDF carry each fact with its citation. Candidates for up to 50 accounts can be pulled in one call on Pro or Team.
  • The deadline rule. The general protest deadline under Tax Code §41.44(a)(1), moved past weekends and legal holidays under §1.06, as a date or a calendar file. See the Texas property tax protest deadline page.

Tools

  • registry_get: a parcel, legal entity or building by FactLot ID, with its status, current cited facts and ownership links. A parcel's value history by tax year is every year with a Pro or Team key and the current roll year without one.

What FactLot is not

FactLot is software. It is not a property tax consultant under Texas Occupations Code chapter 1152, does not prepare, file or advise on protests, and does not represent owners before an appraisal review board. If you provide property tax services to others for compensation, your own registration obligations apply. The candidates follow a stated rule and are for a person to review; FactLot does not choose comparables for a client.

These outputs are not an appraisal or an opinion of value (Occupations Code §1103.003(1)), not legal or tax advice, and not a substitute for the advice of an attorney. They do not say whether to protest, what value to put forward, or how a hearing may go. FactLot does not file protests, sign agent designations under §1.111, or contact appraisal districts. Filing is done through the appraisal district's own process, by the owner or by a property tax consultant registered with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, or an attorney.
