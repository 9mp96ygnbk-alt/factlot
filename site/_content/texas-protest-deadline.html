In Texas, a property owner protests an appraised value by filing a written notice of protest with the appraisal review board (ARB). The general deadline is in Tax Code §41.44(a)(1). FactLot computes it from the statute text, rolls it past weekends and legal holidays, and cites the sections it relied on.

FactLot is software. It is not a property tax consultant under Texas Occupations Code chapter 1152, does not prepare, file or advise on protests, and does not represent owners before an appraisal review board. If you provide property tax services to others for compensation, your own registration obligations apply.

"Statutory deadline" is the last day a notice of protest is timely under the general rule. "Conservative file-by date" is the latest business day on or before the unadjusted date, for filers who do not want to rely on the weekend and holiday rollover.

How the deadline is set

  • The later of two dates. Under §41.44(a)(1) the notice of protest is due by May 15, or by the 30th day after the appraisal district delivered the notice of appraised value, whichever is later. A mailed notice is presumed delivered on the day it was deposited in the mail (§1.07(c)).
  • Weekends and holidays. When the last day falls on a Saturday, Sunday or a legal state or national holiday, §1.06 (as amended by H.B. 1392, effective January 1, 2026) makes the act timely on the next regular business day. The holiday list comes from Government Code §662.003; optional holidays under §662.003(c) are not counted as legal holidays.
  • Commercial property. The general deadline is the same for commercial, industrial and residential property.

What the rule does not cover

The rule computes the general deadline only. It does not cover the other deadlines in §41.44(a)(2) through (5), corrections under §25.25, late-protest relief under §41.44(b) through (c-3), or how a particular appraisal district or ARB handles filings in practice. Check the notice you received and the appraisal district's own instructions. This page is information, not legal or tax advice, and it is not a substitute for the advice of an attorney.

Check a specific notice

If the notice of appraised value arrived late in April or later, the 30-day basis can move the deadline past May 15. An agent can compute the deadline for a specific delivery date with the rules_tx_property_tax_protest_deadline tool (inputs tax_year and notice_delivered_date) over MCP or REST: 

curl -s https://api.factlot.com/v1/rules/tx_property_tax_protest_deadline \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"tax_year":{{TX_NEXT_YEAR}},"notice_delivered_date":"{{TX_NEXT_YEAR}}-04-28"}'

The result names the governing basis (May 15 or the 30-day rule), every skipped weekend day or holiday, the statute citations, and the rule's version. It also gives the deadline's status on a date (as_of, today in US Central time unless given): not_yet_open, open or passed, with days_remaining, and once the deadline has passed the next tax year's deadline under the same rule (next_season).

For consultants, appraisers and analysts

The same tools serve registered property tax consultants, appraisers, attorneys and CRE analysts who run an unequal-appraisal comparison in their own agent, over MCP or REST. Each step names the tool that does it; the person running it chooses the comparables and every adjustment.

  1. Coverage. The hosted API serves Harris, Dallas, Travis, Collin, Denton and Williamson counties (Denton and Williamson from 2027 preliminary rolls); registry_coverage lists exactly what is hosted. Tarrant County is not served by the hosted registry while the publisher's terms for AI use are unclear.
  2. Resolve the parcel. registry_resolve_parcel turns the county and the appraisal district account number on the notice into a FactLot parcel ID.
  3. Value history. The subject that registry_tx_equity_candidates returns carries its market and appraised value by tax year, each cited to that year's roll.
  4. Candidates. registry_tx_equity_candidates returns candidates under FactLot's default rule, a design choice, not a legal standard; a person decides. Its calc_args is that list in the calculator's input shape, for the person to review and edit before the calculation. It returns no owner names, mailing addresses or ownership facts.
  5. Calculator. calc_tx_unequal_appraisal does the arithmetic of Tax Code §41.43(b)(3) and §42.26(a)(3) on the comparables and adjustments the user supplies: each comparable's value, normalized by area if asked and adjusted with a stated reason, then the median of the adjusted values and the subject's value minus that median. It warns when fewer than 5 comparables are given.
  6. PDF packet, workbook and fact package. The hosted report_create (kind tx_unequal_appraisal) builds the evidence packet as a PDF, or the adjustment grid as an XLSX workbook with live Excel formulas beside FactLot's numbers, from the same arguments as the calculator, by code with no model. The link works 24 hours, then the file is deleted. A self-hosted FactLot builds the workbook with examples/reconciliation/build.py tx_unequal_appraisal. fact_package_create bundles the cited roll facts into a package anyone can check with fact_package_verify.

The Claude Code plugin's tx-unequal-appraisal-arithmetic skill runs these steps in order. Registry lookups and reports are metered by plan (3 reports a month free per IP address); see pricing.
