In Texas, a property owner protests an appraised value by filing a written notice of protest with the appraisal review board (ARB). The general deadline is in Tax Code §41.44(a)(1). FactLot computes it from the statute text, rolls it past weekends and legal holidays, and cites the sections it relied on.

"Statutory deadline" is the last day a notice of protest is timely under the general rule. "Conservative file-by date" is the latest business day on or before the unadjusted date, for filers who do not want to rely on the weekend and holiday rollover.

How the deadline is set

  • The later of two dates. Under §41.44(a)(1) the notice of protest is due by May 15, or by the 30th day after the appraisal district delivered the notice of appraised value, whichever is later. A mailed notice is presumed delivered on the day it was deposited in the mail (§1.07(c)).
  • Weekends and holidays. When the last day falls on a Saturday, Sunday or a legal state or national holiday, §1.06 (as amended by H.B. 1392, effective January 1, 2026) makes the act timely on the next regular business day. The holiday list comes from Government Code §662.003; optional holidays under §662.003(c) are not counted as legal holidays.
  • Commercial property. The general deadline is the same for commercial, industrial and residential property.

What the rule does not cover

The rule computes the general deadline only. It does not cover the other deadlines in §41.44(a)(2) through (5), corrections under §25.25, late-protest relief under §41.44(b) through (c-3), or how a particular appraisal district or ARB handles filings in practice. Check the notice you received and the appraisal district's own instructions. This page is information, not legal or tax advice.

Check a specific notice

If the notice of appraised value arrived late in April or later, the 30-day basis can move the deadline past May 15. An agent can compute the deadline for a specific delivery date with the rules_tx_property_tax_protest_deadline tool (inputs tax_year and notice_delivered_date) over MCP or REST: 

curl -s https://api.factlot.com/v1/rules/tx_property_tax_protest_deadline \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"tax_year":{{TX_NEXT_YEAR}},"notice_delivered_date":"{{TX_NEXT_YEAR}}-04-28"}'

The result names the governing basis (May 15 or the 30-day rule), every skipped weekend day or holiday, the statute citations, and the rule's version.
